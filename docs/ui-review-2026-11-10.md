# UI review, 2026-11-10

A review of the whole UI, asked for by the owner: wrong control types for the data, places where icons would simplify
things, and general UI anti-patterns. How it was done:

- **Every built-in device rendered.** All 33 were inserted on a track in a separate dev instance, each control read out
  of its dock card and matched to the type of the parameter it shows (`continuous`, `integer`, `choice`, `toggle`),
  with a screenshot of each.
- **The main views screenshotted:** arrangement, inspector, dock, piano roll, mixer, export dialog.
- **The code searched** for anti-patterns: toggles, control choice by parameter kind, error and info messages, focus
  styles, font sizes, double-click and right-click-only actions.

Findings are grouped and roughly ordered by how much they matter. Each says where it is and what I'd do.

## 1. Wrong control for the data

**1.1 The generic device panel shows every parameter as a rotary knob.** `ui/src/dock/DeviceStrip.tsx:629` maps all
of a node's parameters to `<Knob>`, whatever their kind. Every device without a panel of its own gets it, so:
- the Oscillator (voice graphs) shows **Waveform** (a choice of 5) and **Free phase** (on/off) as knobs. A knob for a
  switch has a full 270° arc with two meaningful positions; a knob for a waveform hides the five choices behind
  "drag until it says Saw";
- private nodes and any future device without a panel get the same.

The right component already exists: `Control` in `dock/MidiEffectPanels.tsx:21` picks a choice slot, a toggle or a
knob by kind. Move it to `dock/` as a shared control and use it in the generic panel. For the Oscillator's waveform,
use a row of waveform icon buttons (see 2.1).

**1.2 Component nodes' exposed parameters are number fields for every kind.** `graph/GraphNodeView.tsx:166`
(`ExposedControl`) uses a `DragNumber` with step 1 for choices and toggles. Dragging a number to pick "Band-pass", or
to turn something on, is a wrong control twice over. Use a `<select>` for choices and a switch for toggles, as
`NodeInspector` does.

**1.3 The inspector's instrument section leaves out choices and toggles.** `components/InstrumentSection.tsx:27` keeps
only `continuous` and `integer` parameters, then takes the first 8. An instrument's main choice (an oscillator's
waveform in a voice instrument, a synth's mode) is never in the inspector, whatever its importance. Choose the
inspector's controls by importance, not kind. A per-type list of "main controls" is better than "the first 8 that are
numbers".

**1.4 The node inspector differs from the dock.** `components/NodeInspector.tsx:65-100`:
- toggles are bare checkboxes, choices full-width selects;
- integers are continuous range sliders with no steps.

The same parameter thus looks and behaves differently in the graph inspector and the dock. Use the same controls
everywhere: one `Control` by kind (1.1).

**1.5 Two-way choices shown as dropdowns.** Opening a menu to pick one of two values, both of which could be visible,
is slower and hides the alternative. These should be two-button segmented controls (or a switch where one value means
"off"):

| Device | Parameter | Values | Better as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arpeggiator, Note Repeat | Velocity | From note / Fixed | segmented |
| Arpeggiator | Retrigger | Off / On new note | switch |
| Chord | Strum order | Up / Down | two arrow icons |
| Scale Lock | Follow | Own / Project key | switch ("Follow project key") |
| Bit Crusher | Clip | Hard / Soft | segmented |
| Bit Crusher | Quantiser, labelled "Steps" | Mid-tread / Mid-rise | segmented (and one name: 1.9) |

Scale Lock's Mode (4 values) and the Phaser's Stages (2–10, even) fit segmented controls as well.

**1.6 Small integer ranges on rotary knobs.** Arpeggiator Octaves (1–4), Chord Inversion, Chorus Voices and the
Oscillator's Unison are integers with a handful of values shown on 270° knobs. The pointer jumps between few positions
and the arc suggests a continuum. Use a stepper (− value +) or a segmented row; keep knobs for wide ranges such as
Coarse (±48 st).

**1.7 Four different toggle styles.**
- `slot-toggle`: an empty 32 px square that fills when on (`styles.css:1419`). Utility's Invert L/R and Mono, the Bit
  Crusher's Dither, the Arpeggiator's Latch, the Convolution Reverb's Reverse/Normalise and the Loop Reverb's Mute use
  it. Off looks like an empty input, and nothing inside it says what it does.
- Text buttons with an active state: Delay Sync/Link/Ping-pong, sidechain HP/LP/Listen, the Feedback node's
  "Above 100 %".
- Checkboxes: the node inspector, dialogs.
- M/S/R letter switches: track headers, the mixer.

Pick one switch for parameters: a button with an icon or short label and a lit state, like the text buttons. Retire
the empty square.

**1.8 Controls that don't apply stay live.** When Velocity is "From note", the Arpeggiator's and Note Repeat's Fixed
velocity knob is still shown and draggable. Delay shows note values when synced (good); check every "mode" parameter
for the controls it makes irrelevant. Disable those (dimmed, not draggable) or hide them; `paramApplies` exists for
this and isn't used for these.

**1.9 One parameter, two names.** The Bit Crusher's quantiser is labelled "Steps" in the panel and "Quantiser" in its
parameter list, automation and the select's title. A parameter should have one name everywhere.

**1.10 Important values only on a graph.**
- Compressor, Gate and Expander: Threshold, Ratio, Hysteresis and Range are set only by dragging handles. The numbers
  under the graph look like readouts and can't be typed.
- The EQ's bands: frequency, gain and Q only on the curve.
- The Filter: cutoff and resonance (readouts exist and take MIDI learn).

The graph is the right main control, but each value also needs a precise way in: make the readouts `DragNumber`s
(drag, double-click to type), as the step sequencer's fields are.

**1.11 Macros: eight dropdowns for a three-way setting.** The modulation column shows each macro's neutral position
(Bottom / Centre / Top) as a full `<select>`, eight of them stacked, plus a name field and a CC field per macro, under
the same eight knobs. Use a small 3-state icon button by each knob (arc from the bottom, the centre or the top), and
fold the names and CC numbers into an "Edit macros" view.

## 2. Icons that would simplify

Icons help where the label is a shape or a direction that the eye reads faster than a word. They need tooltips, and
words stay better for values (Off / 2× / 4×, 1/16).

**2.1 Waveforms and shapes.**
- Chorus, Flanger, Phaser: Sine and Triangle as text buttons. Use waveform glyphs.
- The Oscillator's Waveform: sine, triangle, saw, square, noise.
- The Waveshaper's preset curves (Identity, Soft, Fold, Steps): use small curve thumbnails.
- The Filter's mode select (Low-pass, High-pass, Band-pass, Notch): use the four filter-shape icons as a segmented
  control. Its Slope (12–48 dB/oct) can stay text.

**2.2 Directions.** The Chord's strum order (up/down arrows), the Arpeggiator's Mode (Up, Down, Up-Down, Down-Up,
Up & Down, as arrow patterns), Scale Lock's Mode (nearest/up/down).

**2.3 Common audio functions.**

| Now (text) | Where | Icon |
|---|---|---|
| Sync | Chorus, Flanger, Phaser, Delay, Feedback | a note or a metronome |
| Link | Delay | a chain link |
| Ping-pong | Delay; the label wraps onto two lines in its card | ↔ |
| HP, LP | sidechain sections | filter slopes |
| Listen | sidechain sections | headphones |
| Invert L / Invert R | Utility | Ø |
| Mono | Utility | a single circle |
| Clear | Feedback, step sequencer | an eraser |
| Add row / Remove | step sequencer | + / − |

**2.4 Places where an icon is ambiguous today.**
- **Top bar:** the Metronome icon reads as a pencil at that size.
- **Mixer window:** the "M" and "S" buttons in the top bar have no visible meaning; they need a label or tooltip and a
  clearer form, such as "clear all solos".
- **Modulation column, empty state:** a lone "+" circle under "+ Add macros".
- **Piano roll toolbar:** three icon buttons in a row (drum part, humanise, MIDI effects) need grouping or labels.

## 3. General anti-patterns

**3.1 Text cut off or overlapping.** Choice slots are 58 px wide at most (`styles.css:960`):
- "On new no", "Project key" and "From note" are clipped;
- "Major" runs under the chevron;
- the inspector's "Pressure ..." knob label is cut.

Some choice slots show a chevron and some don't, so the same control looks different. Give slots a minimum width that
fits their longest choice (or fit the text), always show the chevron, and use a tooltip with the full value.

**3.2 Transport order.** The top bar reads Stop, Record, Punch, Pre-roll, Play: Play is at the far end, away from Stop.
DAWs put Play, Stop and Record together (Play first). There's also no loop toggle in the transport; it's only in the
ruler (click its upper half), which few will find.

**3.3 Errors shown twice, and hints shown as errors.**
- An error appears in the red toast and again in red text in the status bar at the same time.
- Hints go through the error toast (`lastError`), in red with a warning icon, though nothing failed:
  - "Only musical files stretch…" (`arrangement/useArrangementEditing.ts:154`);
  - the note-linking rule (`pianoroll/useRollEditing.ts:818`).

Show each message once, and give hints an info style.

**3.4 Hidden actions.** Much is reachable only by double-click or right-click:
- adding key changes, markers and tempo points;
- renaming;
- MIDI learn;
- Map to Macro;
- clip and note operations (18 components have context menus).

Power users expect it, but nothing on screen says it's there, apart from long tooltips. Give the common ones a visible
way in too: a "+" on the key, marker and signature strips, and a small "learn" affordance on hover over a knob.

**3.5 The sound browser's search field says "Add a folder".** With no folder, the browser's search input has the
placeholder "Add a folder", but typing in it searches. Adding a folder is the "+" above it. Show an "Add a folder"
button in the empty state instead, and keep the input for searching.

**3.6 Rarely used settings always on show.** The inspector's Timing offset (value, a tiny "ms" toggle and a "0 smp"
line) is always visible, and the mixer repeats it in every strip ("0 ms", "0smp"). It's a power setting. Put it in a
folded "Advanced" section, or show it only when it isn't 0.

**3.7 Small type.**
- `styles.css` uses 10 px 47 times, 9 px 10 times and 8 px once.
- Knob values are 10 px monospace in a dim colour.
- On a 1080p screen at 100 %, much of the dock is hard to read.

Set a floor (11 px for labels, 12 px for values) and check contrast for the dim text colour.

**3.8 Keyboard focus is often invisible.** `styles.css` has 12 `outline: none` rules and only 4 `:focus-visible`
rules. Tabbing through dialogs and panels loses the focus. Add a `:focus-visible` ring wherever the outline is
removed.

**3.9 Bypass looks different in every view.**
- The dock: a power icon.
- The inspector's insert list: a power icon, plus an × for remove right next to it.
- The mixer's insert list: a filled or hollow dot.

One device's on/off should look the same everywhere. The mixer's dots also tell on from off by fill alone.

**3.10 Colour carries state on its own.** Record arm (a red dot), the automation "R" (green), and the mixer's insert
dots (green or hollow) rely on colour. With red-green colour blindness the arm and automation states are hard to tell
apart. Add a shape or a fill change, such as a filled versus hollow circle, a letter, or a strike-through for
bypassed.

**3.11 Fields that don't look editable.** The step sequencer's step fields (Vel, Len, Chance, Hits, Note, Steps) and
its swing ("50 %", with no label at all) are drag-numbers that look like plain text. Give drag-numbers one visible
style (a subtle box or underline, an ↕ cursor), and label the swing.

**3.12 The piano roll toolbar.** It's one long row of label and value pairs:
- Snap, Swing, Strength, Key, Fold;
- three icon buttons;
- the whole vibrato section (Depth, Rate, Shape, Delay, Fade, Glide), greyed out but still taking space when no note
  is selected.

Group it: grid (snap, swing, strength), view (key, fold), tools (drum, humanise). Show the vibrato fields only when
notes are selected, or in a popover.

**3.13 Vertical text for actions.** The dock's "Choose instrument", "MIDI" and "Add effect" strips use rotated text.
It's hard to read and looks like a label rather than a button. A "+" with a tooltip, or a short horizontal label on a
narrow card, reads better.

**3.14 Inconsistent units and precision.**
- The Oscillator's Phase reads "0.00 deg" while the modulation effects' Spread reads "90.0°".
- Percentages carry two decimals ("0.00%", "100%") next to one ("50.0%").
- The Chord's Strum shows "0.00 ms".

Pick one symbol per unit (°), and round readouts to what can be heard or set, for example 1 % steps and 0.1 ms.

**3.15 Idle display areas take space.** The Arpeggiator's, Chord's and Scale Lock's panels give a third of the card to
a display that says "Hold some keys" or shows an empty keyboard while nothing plays. That's fine at a large dock size.
At the default height it pushes the controls into one cramped row, so let the display shrink first.

**3.16 Export dialog details.**
- Select boxes are shorter than the text fields beside them.
- "Normalise" is a checkbox, a number field and "dBFS" on one line.
- "Real time" sits under a "Mode" heading.

Small things, but the dialog is a first impression. Use equal control heights, and a "Render: Offline / Real time"
segmented control.

## 5. Added after the review

**5.1 Waveforms drawn as bars.** The arrangement and the audio editor draw a file's wave with `timeline/waveform.ts`
(`drawWave`): the peaks' envelope filled as one shape, and the raw frames when zoomed in past the finest peaks. Two
places still draw one 1 px bar per pixel from the peaks:
- the Sampler's sample view (`dock/SampleWaveform.tsx`);
- the sound browser's preview (`components/SoundExplorer.tsx`, `PreviewWave`).

A short drum sample shown across 500 px is about 10 frames a pixel, finer than the finest peaks (256 frames a point),
so each peak becomes a flat run of bars: the staircase in the Sampler. Both should use `drawWave`, which needs to
take a path as well as a pool file id (the browser's files aren't in the pool), and the Sampler's start, end and loop
markers drawn over it as now.

**5.2 The Sampler's layout.** The pads were a fixed 232 px column and small, while the sample view and the envelope
split the rest equally: in the instrument window both were huge, and the envelope, a four-point graph, got as much
room as the waveform. The sound column and "Whole sampler" sat apart at the right. Now, with container queries on the
panel so it reads the same in the dock and the window:

```
+-- Pads ----------+ +-- snare.wav --------------------------+ +-- Pad -----------+
| [] [] [] []      | |   waveform, markers                   | | Vol  Pan  Tune   |
| [] [] [] []      | |                                       | | One  Gate  Loop  |
| [] [] [] []      | +---------------------------------------+ | Choke   Mapping  |
| [] [] [] []      | | envelope (short)       | Attack  0 ms | +-- Sampler -------+
|                  | |                        | Decay   0 ms | | Level  Velocity  |
+------------------+ +---------------------------------------+ +------------------+
```

- **Pads lead and scale.** The grid is square, as tall as the panel (and at most 45 % of its width), up to 520 px.
- **The waveform with the envelope under it** together take the pads' height, so the waveform alone is shorter.
- **The envelope is a strip:** a short graph (48–110 px) with Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release as knobs under it.
  Where the panel is under 360 px tall they are values to type instead; below 180 px the graph goes too.
- **One controls column:** the pad's sound on top, the whole Sampler's Level and Velocity under it.
- **No narrow layout:** the panel is at least 760 px wide. The instrument window and the dock card can't be made
  narrower than that, so the three columns always stay side by side.
- In a very tall window the layout stops at 546 px rather than stretching the waveform.

**5.3 Values inside text, and readouts that don't line up.** Under the dynamics and filter graphs, values sat in
lines of text ("Hysteresis 6.00 dB Range −∞ dB"), wrapped mid-value ("1.00" above "kHz") or started at different
places. Now a shared grid (`components/Readouts.tsx`) shows labels and boxed values in columns: the Compressor, Gate,
Expander and Filter readouts, and the Sampler's envelope values where its knobs don't fit. The step sequencer's
sections have their actions in their headers and two aligned rows of labelled fields. Feedback's limit and shortest
loop are dim status text on one line; Loop Reverb's pattern seed, track headers' gain and pan, and send levels are
boxed fields.

## 4. What works well

- **Device cards:** a consistent header (power, name, presets, collapse), sections with headings, and knobs that show
  their modulation swing.
- **Tooltips:** they explain gestures (drag, Shift for fine, double-click to reset), and every icon button has a title
  or label. The search found none without.
- **The step sequencer's grid:** the step parameters strip, the playhead and the beat grouping read clearly.
- **The macro and modulation column:** chips with hover highlighting of what a source drives is a good model.

## Suggested order

1. 1.1–1.4: one `Control` by kind, used by the generic panel, the graph node, the node inspector and the instrument
   section. Mechanical, and it fixes every case of a knob for a switch.
2. 3.1, 3.11 and 3.7: truncation, drag-number affordance and small type. Visible everywhere, cheap.
3. 1.5–1.8: segmented controls and steppers, one switch style, disabled when irrelevant.
4. 2.1–2.3: icons, starting with waveforms, filter shapes and Sync/Link/Listen.
5. 3.2–3.6 and 3.12: transport order, messages, discoverability, the piano roll toolbar.

## Dragging number fields (2026-11-12)

A field's step used to be both how far a pixel of drag moved it and what it rounded to, set by hand for each of 34
fields: a modulation depth crossed its range in 2000 px, Unison went through its 16 voices in 15. Now one rule
(`ui/src/components/dragScale.ts`) derives the drag from the range, and a field only says what it rounds to and its
curve:

- a continuous value crosses its range in 200 px (a knob turns in 160), along its parameter's curve where it has one,
  so a skewed Hz or ms field gives the low end the travel its knob does; gains follow the fader's curve, a tempo a log
  curve over 1000 to 1200 px (about 2 px a BPM around 120), times and crossfades a curve fine at their short end, an
  offset fine near zero;
- a whole number moves at least 10 px a step (up to 20 for a short range), its whole range in no more than 300 px:
  Unison 13 px a voice, a row's 64 steps 5 px, a velocity 2.4 px;
- Shift is ten times finer (for a whole number, down to 10 px a step), as in Bitwig and Cubase, and pressing or
  letting go of it mid-drag carries on from where the value is, without a jump;
- a wheel notch is a step of a whole number or a hundredth of a continuous value's travel.

The `step` prop is gone, so a field can't get a speed of its own again.

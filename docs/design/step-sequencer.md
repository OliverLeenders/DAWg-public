# Step sequencer

Status: accepted (owner, 2026-11-09), built.

A step sequencer is a device that makes notes from a grid of steps, not a view of a MIDI clip. Its patterns live in
the device and loop on their own length; clips in the arrangement only say where it plays and which pattern. This is
the last of the "MIDI and composing tools" (docs/PLAN.md, after phase 21).

## Decisions

**SS1. A device of the MIDI effect kind.** `dawg.stepSequencer` sits in the track's MIDI section (docs/design/
midi-effects.md, ME2), first by default, so an Arpeggiator, Chord or Scale Lock after it processes what it plays.
Notes coming in (the track's MIDI clips, live input) pass through unchanged and play alongside its own. One per track.
It is refused in voice graphs and on audio tracks, like the other MIDI effects.

**SS2. Sequencer clips gate it.** A clip with a sequencer pattern (`NoteClip::sequencerPattern` ≥ 0) holds no notes:
the sequencer plays while the playhead is inside it, and only there (the owner: "a step sequencer's clip length in the
arranger should basically correspond to the time interval in which midi output is generated by the sequencer"). It
moves, resizes, splits, duplicates and stacks in takes lanes like any clip; its loop range is not used.

**SS3. The loop starts at step 1 at the clip's start**, unless the clip names another start step
(`NoteClip::sequencerStartStep`, owner). Steps count from the clip's content origin (its start minus its offset), so
trimming the left edge leaves the steps where they were and the right half of a split clip carries on where the left
half stopped.

**SS3a. Steps are chased (2026-11-12).** When the transport starts, jumps or loops, a step that began before that point
and still sounds starts at once, as a clip's notes are chased. Without it a track processed ahead of the playhead (a
Limiter's look-ahead, a plugin's latency) first saw its clip from the latency on, and the step at the clip's start
never played.

**SS4. Patterns.** The device holds up to 16 patterns (A to P); each sequencer clip picks one. Variations and fills are
clips side by side.

**SS5. Rows.** The device's rows are shared by its patterns: each row plays one pitch and has a name. A new device
takes its rows from the track's Sampler (one per zone key, up to 16) or else from a General MIDI drum kit. Rows can be
added, removed and set to any pitch, so melodic rows work as well; the panel offers the project key's scale notes when
adding one.

**SS6. Steps.** Each pattern has a step size (1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, or the triplets 1/8T, 1/16T, 1/32T), a swing
(50–75 %, the quantise swing's math, docs/design/midi-effects.md, ME18) and, per row, a length of 1 to 64 steps
(polymeters: rows of different lengths drift against each other). Each step holds on or off, velocity, length (in
steps, 0.1 to 16, so a note can tie over the following steps), chance (0–100 %) and ratchet (1 to 4 hits in the step).

**SS7. Chance is repeatable.** Whether a step with a chance below 100 % plays is decided by a hash of the clip, the step
count from the clip's start, the row and the hit: the same every time the song plays and when it is exported, and
different on every pass through the pattern.

**SS8. Without a clip it is silent while playing.** While the transport is stopped, the panel's Play button, or a
held key, previews the shown pattern on its own clock (not undoable, not saved); starting the transport stops the
preview.

**SS9. Editing in the device panel.** The dock panel shows the pattern's grid: rows down, steps across, a velocity bar
in each lit step. Click toggles a step, drag paints, Alt+drag sets velocity; the selected step's length, chance and
ratchet are in a strip below. The pattern choice, step size, swing, row lengths and Play are in the panel's controls.

**SS10. Render to MIDI clip.** The clip menu's Render steps to MIDI replaces a sequencer clip with a MIDI clip of the
notes it plays (chance decided as in SS7), one undo step.

**SS11. Commands.** `setStepCell`, `setStepRow`, `addStepRow`, `removeStepRow`, `setStepPattern`, `copyStepPattern`,
`clearStepPattern` edit the device; `addClip` takes `sequencer: { pattern, startStep }`, `setClipSequencer` changes a
clip's pattern and start step, and `renderStepClip` renders one.

## Implementation order

1. Model: the device's data on its graph node, sequencer clip fields, the commands, the file; tests.
2. Engine: the node and the clips' spans in the track sequence; tests for timing, start step, polymeters, chance,
   ratchets, block-size independence, no hung notes, no allocation.
3. UI: the device panel, sequencer clips in the arrangement (adding, the pattern and start step), render to MIDI clip.

## Notes from building it

- `engine/include/dawg/StepSequencer.h` works out the hits (`stepseq::forEachStepHit`); the node, Render steps to MIDI
  and the tests use it, and `ui/src/dock/stepGrid.ts` mirrors it to draw sequencer clips in the arrangement.
- The node is a MIDI effect: its own notes are pending notes of the effect base (`startOwn`), placed for each block
  from the sequencer clips the track sequence lists (`TrackSequence::sequencerSpans`). The steps reach it through
  `configure()`, so editing a pattern never recompiles.
- On a track with a step sequencer, a clip drawn or added in the arrangement is a sequencer clip on pattern A;
  double-clicking one selects the track, so the device strip shows the sequencer. The clip menu picks the pattern and
  start step and has Render steps to MIDI. Render MIDI effects to clip leaves the step sequencer out.
- Rows from a Sampler are named after the first zone's sample on each key; the General MIDI kit is used without a
  Sampler.
- While stopped, a held live key (a MIDI keyboard, or the piano roll's keyboard) plays the pattern the panel shows,
  until every key is let go; the key itself passes through too. The panel tells the device which pattern it shows
  (`stepSequencer.shown`).
- The grid has a playhead: while playing, the step of the sequencer clip under the playhead when it plays the shown
  pattern, on each row by its own length; while previewing, the preview's clock (estimated in the panel).
- Checked in the app (a separate dev instance): inserting the device, painting steps with the mouse, the panel's
  sections at the default dock height without overflow, two sequencer clips (one from step 5) drawn with their
  steps, the preview sounding while stopped, and rendering a clip to notes. Not checked by ear.
- Checked in the app later: a held audition key sounding the pattern while stopped and silence after letting go, and
  the grid's playhead while playing.
